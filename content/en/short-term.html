Ideas for a day on two wheels

With a bike, the city opens up beyond the Old Quarter. A few easy rides to start with:

  • West Lake. Follow the lakeside roads, stop at Tran Quoc Pagoda and find a café with a view over the water.
  • Ba Dinh and the Temple of Literature. The Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum area and the temple, often called Vietnam's first university, are a short ride from the Old Quarter.
  • The French Quarter. Wide, tree-lined streets and colonial buildings around the Opera House, just south-east of Hoan Kiem Lake.
  • The Vietnam Museum of Ethnology. A longer ride west of the centre, worth it for the full-size traditional houses in its outdoor exhibition.

Most sights have paid motorbike parking nearby. On your first ride, plan the route before you set off and stay out of rush hour.

Collecting the bike

Hand-over takes place near the Old Quarter, at a time and place arranged with you by phone. Call ahead rather than just turning up, especially on the day you arrive, and allow a few minutes for the documents and the condition check before you ride off.

Your rental runs from the hand-over time to the return time written in your agreement, so agree a return time that fits your onward plans, whether that is an early bus or a night train. If your first day in Hanoi is already full, it can make sense to collect the bike the following morning instead.

Extending your rental

Plans change, and Hanoi has a way of making people stay longer. To keep the bike for extra days, contact Mr Tu before your return time. If the bike is free, Mr Tu confirms the new return date and the price, and the change is noted in your agreement. If your total rental reaches a week, ask whether the weekly rate applies.

Returning the bike

Bring the bike back at the time and place written in your agreement. You check it over together, using the same points as at hand-over, and the fuel level is compared with what was noted when you collected it. Once everything matches the agreement, your deposit is refunded under its terms.

Returning early or late

If you need to bring the bike back early, tell Mr Tu as soon as you know. Whether any rent is refunded for unused days depends on your rental agreement, so check this point before you sign if your plans might change.

Running late? Call before the agreed return time. Any charge for a late return is set out in the agreement, and a quick call lets Mr Tu plan around it.

Booking around holidays

If your trip falls around Tet (Lunar New Year) or another public holiday, contact Mr Tu well ahead. Many people in Vietnam travel over the holidays, and a bike is only reserved for your dates once Mr Tu has confirmed it.

Before you ride off: a quick checklist

  • Licence documents. Your national licence with a motorcycle category plus an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention, or a Vietnamese licence. Carry them every time you ride; how to rent a motorbike in Hanoi explains the rules.
  • The agreement. Check the price, deposit, rental period, and return time and place, then keep your copy.
  • The bike. Test the brakes, lights, horn and mirrors with Mr Tu, look at the tyres, photograph any existing marks and note the fuel level. Our step-by-step bike check shows what to look for.
  • Helmets. One for each rider, meeting the national standard, with the strap fastened.
  • Your phone. Save Mr Tu's number and keep a map app ready for the Old Quarter's one-way streets.
  • Small notes for parking. Paid parking (gửi xe) is common at sights and markets, so keep some cash handy.

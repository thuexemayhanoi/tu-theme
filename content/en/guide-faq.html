Motorbike licence rules in Vietnam (as of October 2026)

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, sets out which licence you need. This is what it means for riders.

Licence classes

  • A1: two-wheel motorcycles up to 125 cc, or electric motorcycles up to 11 kW.
  • A: motorcycles above 125 cc or 11 kW. Class A also covers A1 bikes.
  • Minimum age: 18 for both A1 and A.
  • Car licences: a car-only licence does not cover motorcycles.

Licences valid for foreign visitors

You can ride legally with either:

  • a Vietnamese licence of the right class, or
  • an International Driving Permit (IDP) issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic, carried together with your valid national licence for the same class. The IDP on its own is not enough.

An IDP issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention is not covered by this rule. Countries differ in which type they issue, so check which convention your permit was issued under before you travel, and if the document doesn't make it clear, ask the authority that issued it.

Mopeds up to 50 cc

Mopeds of up to 50 cc do not need a motorcycle licence class, but riders must be at least 16 and follow traffic law. Standard rental scooters and semi-automatics, such as the Honda Vision or Honda Wave, are above 50 cc, so the A1 and A rules apply to them.

Helmets

Rider and passenger must both wear a helmet that meets Vietnam's national standard, with the strap fastened, on every ride.

Insurance

Riding without third-party insurance is illegal, and rental bikes carry this compulsory cover. It deals with harm you cause to other people, not your own injuries, so check that your travel insurance covers motorbike riding and ask your insurer whether that cover depends on holding the right licence.

Hand-over checklist

Take a few minutes over this with Mr Tu. A careful check protects both of you.

  • Brakes: squeeze front and rear; the bike should stop firmly and smoothly.
  • Tyres: look for good tread and no cuts, cracks or bulges, and ask for the pressure to be checked if a tyre looks soft.
  • Lights: headlight (low and high beam), brake light and both indicators.
  • Horn: you will use it, so make sure it works.
  • Mirrors: both fitted, tight and adjusted for you.
  • Fuel level: written into the agreement when you set off and checked again when you return.
  • Existing marks: photograph the bike from every side, get close-ups of any scratches or dents, and make sure they are noted.
  • Helmets: a good fit for each rider, with a working strap.
  • Paperwork: price, deposit, period, return time and place, and any delivery cost, all in writing. Keep a copy or a photo of it.

Staying safe and legal on the road

Some of these are legal requirements, the rest are simply good sense in Hanoi's traffic:

  • Wear a standard helmet with the strap done up on every trip, and make sure your passenger does too.
  • Have your licence and IDP with you whenever you ride, not back at the hotel.
  • Drink-driving is a serious offence in Vietnam, so leave the bike parked if you have had any alcohol.
  • Stop at red lights and obey one-way signs, even when others don't.
  • At junctions, expect vehicles from every direction, including the occasional bike coming the wrong way close to the kerb.
  • After dark, ride with your lights on, dipped in town.
  • In a downpour, slow right down or wait it out: painted lines and metal covers get slippery.
  • If it all feels like too much, pull over, park and take a break. A taxi can always cover the rest of the day.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a licence to rent a motorbike in Vietnam?

Yes. Any motorbike above 50 cc requires a licence that covers motorcycles: Vietnamese class A1 or A, or a 1968 Vienna Convention IDP together with your national motorcycle licence. Mr Tu checks your licence documents for the rental agreement.

Can tourists rent a motorbike in Vietnam?

Yes, as long as you hold a valid licence for motorcycles. For most visitors, that means a 1968-Convention IDP plus a national motorcycle licence. Without it, you would be riding illegally, and your travel insurance may not cover you.

Is my International Driving Permit valid in Vietnam?

Only if it was issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic and you carry it with your valid national licence for the same class. A permit issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention doesn't qualify, so look at the convention printed on yours before you travel.

Can I ride a motorbike in Vietnam with a car licence?

No. A car-only licence, or an IDP that only covers cars, does not cover motorcycles. Your licence needs a motorcycle category matching the bike (A1 or A).

Can I ride a 50 cc moped without a motorcycle licence?

A 50 cc moped needs no motorcycle licence class, but you must be 16 or older and ride within traffic law. That exception doesn't extend to ordinary rental scooters and semi-automatics, which have bigger engines.

How old do I have to be to ride a motorbike in Vietnam?

18 for a motorcycle licence (class A1 or A). Riders of 50 cc mopeds must be at least 16.

What documents do I need to rent from Mr Tu?

An identity document, such as your passport, and your licence documents. They are checked for the rental agreement, and Mr Tu confirms exactly what to bring when you contact us.

Should I leave my passport as a deposit?

No, and Mr Tu won't ask you to. British government travel advice warns against it, so your passport is only checked for the agreement and the deposit is settled separately, for example in cash.

Can I rent without paying a deposit?

No. A deposit is part of every rental with Mr Tu. It is kept separate from the rental price, varies by model and is confirmed with you in advance.

When do I get my deposit back?

When you return the bike at the agreed time and place and you've checked it together, under the terms of your rental agreement. If something is damaged, only the actual cost of that damage is taken into account.

Do I have to wear a helmet?

Yes, and so does your passenger, on every ride. The helmet must meet Vietnam's national standard and be worn with the strap fastened. When you book, tell Mr Tu how many people will ride so that helmets for everyone can be confirmed.

Is insurance included?

Only the compulsory third-party cover that every bike in Vietnam must carry. It pays for harm you cause to others, not for your own injuries or medical bills, so make sure your travel insurance includes motorbike riding.

Who pays for traffic fines?

You do. Any fine for an offence committed while the bike is rented to you is the renter's responsibility, as the rental agreement states.

What if the bike breaks down or I have an accident?

Make sure you and everyone else are safe first. In an emergency, dial 113 (police) or 115 (ambulance), then let Mr Tu know. Don't have the bike repaired without agreeing it with Mr Tu; how damage is handled is set out in your agreement.

Is it safe to ride a motorbike in Hanoi?

It can be, if you're an experienced, licensed rider who takes it slowly. Traffic is dense and behaves differently from home, so wear your helmet, never ride after drinking, avoid rush hour at first and stay off the road in heavy rain if you can.

Can I take the bike outside Hanoi?

Ask before you plan it. Where you may ride the bike is agreed with Mr Tu in advance and written into your rental agreement.

Can I carry a passenger?

Yes, provided your passenger also wears a helmet that meets the national standard, with the strap fastened. Mention them when you book, and expect the bike to brake and corner differently with two people on board.

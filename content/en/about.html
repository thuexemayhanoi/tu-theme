Who we are

Mr Tu is a local rental service based near the Old Quarter (Phố Cổ), next to Hoan Kiem. We rent bikes to travellers exploring Hanoi and to people living here for a while, whether that means a single day out, a week of sightseeing or a month of everyday riding.

The models, prices and contact details on this website are kept in one place and shown the same way on every page, in every language. Whatever you read here, the final word is the confirmation you receive before booking and the agreement you sign at hand-over.

What we rent

We rent the two kinds of bike you see everywhere in Hanoi: automatic scooters, which have no gears and are the easier choice in city traffic, and semi-automatics, where you shift with your foot but have no clutch to manage. The current models are listed on our scooters and motorbikes page, and Mr Tu confirms which one is free for your dates when you call.

How we work

Confirmed before you book

Availability, the rental price, the deposit and the hand-over details are confirmed before anything is agreed. The price and the deposit are separate amounts, and the deposit depends on the model.

Documents checked, not kept

We look at your passport or national ID and your licence to complete the rental agreement, and you keep them. The deposit is a separate, lawful arrangement, such as cash, so you never have to leave your passport behind.

Clear about other costs

Fuel, parking and any traffic fines during your rental are yours to pay. We say so before you book, and your agreement spells out how each one is handled.

Checked together at hand-over

We go over the brakes, tyres, lights, horn and mirrors with you and note how much fuel is in the tank. Photos of the bike's condition mean both sides start from the same record.

Written down

Your rental agreement or receipt lists the price, deposit and rental period, the bike's condition and any extra costs we agreed. If something matters to you and isn't on it, ask us to add it before you sign.

Returned fairly

At the agreed time and place we check the bike together again, and the deposit is refunded as set out in the agreement. If anything is damaged, it is assessed on the actual damage and under the terms you signed.

What we will and won't promise

Trust comes from promises that hold up, so we keep ours short and specific.

We will

  • Confirm the price, deposit and hand-over details before you book.
  • Check the bike with you and photograph its condition.
  • Put what we agreed in writing.
  • Answer questions about documents and licences honestly, even when the answer is that what you hold is not enough to ride legally.
  • Remind you that helmets are compulsory for rider and passenger, on every ride.

We won't

  • Tell you that a licence doesn't matter.
  • Ask you to leave your passport as a deposit.
  • Promise a bike or a price before checking it for your dates.
  • Change what we agreed once you arrive for hand-over.
  • Use slogans we can't back up. We would rather show you exactly how renting works.

Who should rent, and who should wait

Renting a bike is a wonderful way to see Hanoi if you are a confident rider with a licence that covers motorcycles. If you have never ridden before, we would rather tell you honestly that this city's traffic makes a hard first lesson than simply hand you the keys.

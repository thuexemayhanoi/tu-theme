Getting to know the Old Quarter on two wheels

The Old Quarter is a tight grid of short streets just north of Hoan Kiem Lake, many of them named after the goods once traded there. It is busy from early morning until late at night, and riding here is slow, stop-and-go work. A few things are worth knowing before your first ride.

Narrow lanes and one-way streets

Many streets are one-way, and the direction can change from one block to the next. Pavements double as shopfronts, kitchens and parking, so riders, pedestrians and street vendors share the road. Follow the signs rather than your map alone, ride slowly and expect people to step out without looking.

The weekend walking street around Hoan Kiem Lake

On weekend evenings, the streets around Hoan Kiem Lake and several streets in the Old Quarter usually close to traffic and become a walking street. Plan your route around it, park outside the closed area, and if your hand-over or return falls on a weekend evening, check the details with Mr Tu.

Finding your way

Streets here are short, and many change name after a block or two, so it is easy to miss a turn. Plan the route while you are parked, and look at it again only after you have pulled over. Busy times

Weekday rush hours, public holidays and festival nights fill the streets. Take your first ride at a quieter time of day, get a feel for the bike, and save the busy evenings for when you are more confident.

Parking in the Old Quarter

In the centre, the usual way to park is attended, paid parking. Look for a sign saying "gửi xe" (vehicle parking): it may be a courtyard, a shop front or a marked section of pavement with an attendant.

  • Take the ticket and keep it — the attendant hands you a ticket or marks a number on the seat, and you need it to get the bike back.
  • Check the fee — if no price is posted, ask before you leave the bike.
  • Don't park where it isn't allowed — parking on the pavement or roadside where signs forbid it can lead to a fine, and traffic fines are the renter's responsibility.
  • Plan for the night — ask your hotel or guesthouse whether there is space for a motorbike overnight, and on what terms.
  • Take valuables with you — don't leave bags, phones or helmets hanging on the bike.
  • Brief stops — where there is no attendant, lock the steering, take the key and don't leave the bike for long.

Riding out of the centre

Once you reach the wider roads around the Old Quarter, the rest of Hanoi opens up. A few pointers for your first rides out:

  • West Lake — head north-west towards Tay Ho for lakeside roads, cafés and calmer riding than in the centre.
  • The French Quarter — south of Hoan Kiem Lake, broad tree-lined streets make an easy first loop.
  • Across the Red River — several bridges lead east over the river. Check that the one on your route is open to motorbikes before you set off.
  • Ring roads and expressways — some expressways and elevated sections are closed to motorbikes. Watch the signs, and use your map app's motorbike mode if it has one.
  • Beyond Hanoi — if you plan to ride further, tell Mr Tu before you book; where the bike may go is part of what we agree in writing.

Whatever the route, check the fuel level before you leave the centre and fill up on the main roads rather than waiting until the tank is nearly empty.

Hand-over is arranged by phone, a short way from the Old Quarter's busiest streets. Please call before you come, so your bike is checked and someone is there to meet you.

Old Quarter rental: frequently asked questions

Is Mr Tu inside the Old Quarter?

Mr Tu is near the Old Quarter, next to Hoan Kiem, rather than in the middle of its busiest streets. The exact hand-over point and time are confirmed when you call, so please call before you come.

Can I ride through the Old Quarter on weekend evenings?

Only partly. At weekends the lakeside streets and part of the Old Quarter usually close to motorbikes in the evening, so ride around that zone and park outside it.

Where should I park when I visit the Old Quarter?

Use attended, paid parking marked "gửi xe", keep your ticket until you collect the bike, and take your valuables with you.

Is the Old Quarter a hard place for a first ride?

It is busy rather than fast. If you are new to Hanoi, choose an automatic scooter, ride at a quiet time of day and keep your speed low. The flow feels far more natural after a few short rides.

Documents and licence: what you need

As of October 2026, Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025) sets these rules for riders:

  • Licence classes — class A1 covers motorcycles up to 125 cc (or electric motors up to 11 kW); class A covers larger ones. Both are issued from age 18.
  • Licences for visitors — a Vietnamese licence, or an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention, carried together with your valid national licence for the same class. An IDP issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention is not covered, so check which convention yours follows.
  • Car licences — a car-only licence does not cover motorbikes.
  • Helmets — compulsory for rider and passenger. The helmet must meet the national standard and be worn with the strap fastened.
  • Insurance — riding without third-party insurance is illegal, and rental bikes carry this compulsory cover. It does not protect you or the bike, so check that your travel insurance covers motorbike riding.

For the rental itself, bring your passport or national ID and your licence, plus your IDP if you use one. They are checked for the rental agreement; the deposit is agreed separately, in a lawful form such as cash. Mr Tu confirms each model's engine size when you contact us, so you can match the bike to your licence class.

Before you ride off: a quick checklist

  • Squeeze both brakes and check that the bike stops cleanly.
  • Look at both tyres for wear and pressure.
  • Test the headlight, brake light, indicators and horn.
  • Adjust the mirrors to your riding position.
  • Note the fuel level, and photograph any existing scratches or dents.
  • Try on the helmet and fasten the strap: it should sit snugly and not tip forward.
  • Save Mr Tu's number in your phone before you leave.

Riding safely in Hanoi

Hanoi traffic looks chaotic at first, but it follows its own rhythm. These habits help you ride with it rather than against it:

  • Helmet on, strap fastened — for rider and passenger, on every ride, however short.
  • Go with the flow — hold a steady line and speed, and avoid sudden swerves or stops. Other riders steer around you based on what you are doing now.
  • Use the horn to be seen — a short tap at junctions or when overtaking simply says "I'm here". It isn't considered rude.
  • Read the signs, not just the map — many central streets are one-way, and some roads are closed to motorbikes.
  • Avoid rush hour at first — weekday mornings and late afternoons are the busiest times, so take your first rides outside them.
  • Slow down in the rain — roads become slippery and visibility drops. Brake early and carry a rain poncho.
  • Keep your documents on you — carry your licence, and your IDP if you use one, every time you ride.
  • Never drink and ride — drink-driving is a serious offence in Vietnam.
  • Park where it's allowed — use attended, paid parking in the centre and keep the ticket.

Can tourists rent a motorbike in Hanoi?

Yes, as long as they hold a licence that is valid for motorcycles in Vietnam: a Vietnamese A1 or A licence, or an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Convention together with their national motorcycle licence. Mr Tu checks your documents for the rental agreement.

Choose an automatic scooter if you want the simplest ride in city traffic. Choose a semi-automatic if you have ridden a geared bike before and enjoy changing gear yourself. If you're unsure, Mr Tu can suggest a model when you call.

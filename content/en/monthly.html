Choosing a bike for everyday life

Think about how you'll actually use it:

  • Commuting across town. An automatic scooter is the simplest choice in rush-hour traffic.
  • Shopping and passengers. A scooter's under-seat space takes the shopping, and two people fit comfortably for trips around town.
  • Used to gears? A semi-automatic gives you more control and is what many Hanoians ride every day.

Servicing and repairs during your rental

A bike you ride every day needs routine care. Who arranges servicing, such as oil changes, and who pays for it is agreed with Mr Tu and written into your rental agreement before you start, so there's no guesswork later.

Simple checks between services

  • Look at the tyres every few days. If one seems soft, roadside repair stalls all over Hanoi can top up the air.
  • Check the lights and indicators now and then, especially before riding at night.
  • Tell Mr Tu early if something feels wrong: soft brakes, a new noise, a warning light or a worn tyre.

If something goes wrong

  • Don't have the bike repaired or modified without agreeing it with Mr Tu first.
  • If the bike breaks down, move somewhere safe, then call Mr Tu.
  • After an accident, make sure everyone is safe, call 113 (police) or 115 (ambulance) if needed, then let Mr Tu know.

Renting or buying?

Some long-stayers consider buying a bike. That means sorting out the ownership paperwork, judging the condition of a used machine and selling it again before you leave. Renting by the month skips all of that: you agree the terms in writing, ride for as long as you need and hand the bike back when you go.

Documents for a long stay

The documents are the same however long you rent: an identity document, checked for the rental agreement, and a licence that covers motorcycles. For visitors, that means a Vietnamese licence, or an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention carried with your national licence. If you're here for months, make sure both stay valid for your whole stay.

Mr Tu confirms exactly which documents are needed for a long rental when you contact us. Our guide to renting a motorbike in Hanoi explains the licence rules in detail.

Budgeting for a month

When you work out what a bike will cost you each month, include more than the rent:

  • Rent: the monthly rate for your model, from the table above.
  • Deposit: a separate amount you need at the start. It is held for the rental and refunded at the end under your agreement.
  • Fuel: whatever you use. A daily commute adds up over a month.
  • Parking: many apartment buildings and offices charge for motorbike parking, and you'll pay for parking (gửi xe) at markets and sights.
  • Insurance and fines: check that your travel or health insurance covers motorbike riding, and remember that traffic fines are the renter's responsibility.

Parking where you live

Before you sign a lease, ask your landlord or building manager where residents park motorbikes and whether there's a monthly charge. Apartment blocks often have basement parking with a monthly card, while in many older houses bikes are wheeled into the ground floor at night. When you park anywhere else overnight, use attended parking and always lock the steering.

Riding through Hanoi's seasons

Over a month or more, you'll see Hanoi's weather change. Summer brings sudden, heavy downpours, so keep a raincoat under the seat and allow extra braking distance on wet roads. Winter mornings can be cold and damp, and a windproof jacket and gloves make the commute far more pleasant.

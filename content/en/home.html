Start your Hanoi rides from the Old Quarter

Think of the Old Quarter as your kilometre zero. Its tight grid of streets, many still named after the goods once traded there, sits just north of Hoan Kiem Lake, and from here West Lake, the French Quarter and the banks of the Red River are all a short ride away.

Riding in the centre takes patience rather than speed. Lanes are narrow, many streets are one-way and traffic moves as one steady stream. Take your first ride at a quiet time of day, hold a predictable line and give a light tap of the horn at junctions so others know you are there.

Because Mr Tu is close by, collecting and returning your bike doesn't mean a trip across town. Call ahead and we'll arrange the hand-over time and place. Parking, the weekend walking street and routes out of the centre are covered in our guide to motorbike rental in Hanoi's Old Quarter.

How renting works, in five steps

  1. Choose — Pick a model and your dates. If you're unsure, tell us how you plan to ride and we'll suggest a bike.
  2. Contact — Call Mr Tu with the model, your dates and the number of riders. We check availability for you.
  3. Confirm documents and deposit — Mr Tu confirms the price, the deposit, the documents to bring and the hand-over details. Nothing is booked until you agree.
  4. Inspect and ride — We check the bike together (brakes, tyres, lights, horn, mirrors and fuel level), photograph its condition and sign the written agreement. Then the road is yours.
  5. Return — Bring the bike back at the agreed time and place. After a joint check, your deposit is refunded as set out in the agreement.

The practical details, up front

Documents

Bring your passport or national ID and your driving licence. They are checked for the rental agreement; your passport is not used as the deposit.

Deposit

The deposit is separate from the rental price and depends on the model. Its amount and form, such as cash, are agreed before you book, and it is refunded under your agreement.

Licence and helmets

A bike over 50 cc needs a licence that covers motorcycles: a Vietnamese A1 or A licence, or a 1968-Convention IDP carried with your national licence. Helmets are compulsory for rider and passenger.

Agreed in writing

Your agreement records the price, deposit, rental period, the bike's condition and fuel level, and any extra costs you agreed. If it matters to you, it belongs on paper.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a licence to rent a motorbike in Hanoi?

Yes. For any bike over 50 cc you need a Vietnamese A1 or A licence, or an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention carried together with your national motorcycle licence. A car-only licence does not cover motorbikes.

Is the deposit included in the rental price?

No, the two are separate. The deposit depends on the model, Mr Tu confirms the amount before you book, and it is refunded as set out in your rental agreement when you return the bike.

Do you keep my passport?

No. Your passport or ID is checked for the rental agreement, and the deposit is agreed separately in a lawful form such as cash. UK government travel advice also warns travellers against leaving a passport as a deposit.

What about helmets?

Helmets that meet the Vietnamese standard, worn with the strap fastened, are compulsory for both rider and passenger. Tell us how many riders there are when you book so Mr Tu can confirm helmets, and check the fit at hand-over.

Can I rent a motorbike by the month?

Yes. Mr Tu rents by the day, week or month, and a monthly rental suits a longer stay in Hanoi. See monthly motorbike rental in Hanoi for how it works.

How do I book a bike?

Call Mr Tu with the model you'd like, your dates and the number of riders. We confirm availability, price, deposit and hand-over details, and the booking is set once you agree to them.

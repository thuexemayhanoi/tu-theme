Start your Hanoi rides from the Old Quarter

Think of the Old Quarter as your kilometre zero. Hanoi's old merchant district sits beside Hoan Kiem Lake, and from its edge you can reach the lakeside cafés of Tay Ho, the boulevards of the French Quarter or the dyke road along the Red River without crossing the whole city first.

Riding in the centre takes patience rather than speed. Lanes are narrow, many streets are one-way and traffic moves as one steady stream, so plan your first ride for a quiet time of day. Our guide to motorbike rental in Hanoi has more habits that help you ride safely.

Because Mr Tu is close by, collecting and returning your bike doesn't mean a trip across town. Call ahead and we'll arrange the hand-over time and place. Parking, the weekend walking street and routes out of the centre are covered on our page about motorbike rental near Hanoi's Old Quarter.

How renting works, in five steps

  1. Choose — Pick a model and your dates. If you're unsure, tell us how you plan to ride and we'll suggest a bike.
  2. Contact — Call Mr Tu with the model, your dates and the number of riders. We check availability for you.
  3. Confirm documents and deposit — Mr Tu confirms the price, the deposit, the documents to bring and the hand-over details. Nothing is booked until you agree.
  4. Inspect and ride — We check the bike together (brakes, tyres, lights, horn, mirrors and fuel level), photograph its condition and sign the written agreement. Then the road is yours.
  5. Return — Bring the bike back when and where we agreed. Once we have checked it together, your deposit is refunded as the agreement sets out.

The practical details, up front

Documents

Bring your passport or national ID and your driving licence. We check them for the rental agreement, and your passport is never held as the deposit.

Deposit

Set per model and kept apart from the rental price. Its amount and form, such as cash, are agreed before you book.

Licence and helmets

Bikes over 50 cc need a motorcycle licence: a Vietnamese A1 or A, or your national licence plus an IDP issued under the 1968 Convention. Rider and passenger must both wear a helmet.

Agreed in writing

Your agreement records the price, deposit, rental period, the bike's condition and fuel level, and any extra costs you agreed. If it matters to you, it belongs on paper.

Frequently asked questions

Can I ride with the licence from my home country?

Only if it covers motorcycles and you carry it together with an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention. A Vietnamese A1 or A licence also works, but a car-only licence does not.

Do you keep my passport?

No. Your passport or ID is checked for the rental agreement, and the deposit is agreed separately in a lawful form such as cash. UK government travel advice also warns travellers against leaving a passport as a deposit.

Do I need riding experience?

Yes. Come to Hanoi's traffic as a confident rider with the right licence, not as a learner. If you are new to Vietnam's roads, an automatic scooter and a first ride at a quiet hour make the start much easier.

Can I rent a motorbike by the month?

Yes. Mr Tu rents by the day, week or month, and a monthly rental suits a longer stay in Hanoi. The Monthly tab in the price table above shows the current rates.

How do I book a bike?

Call Mr Tu with the model you'd like, your dates and the number of riders. We confirm availability, price, deposit and hand-over details, and your booking stands once you agree to them.
